6 Free AI Writing Tools in ToolForge AI

By ToolForge AI · 2026-10-06 · 4 min read

Six tools on ToolForge AI run on an AI model instead of fixed rules: a prompt generator, a summarizer, a rewriter, a caption generator, an email writer, and a blog outline generator. Each is useful for a different job, and each works better if you know what to feed it. Here is what each one is actually for, in plain terms.

AI Prompt Generator — turn a rough idea into a usable prompt

Use this when you already know roughly what you want from an image or text AI tool, but your first attempt keeps coming back generic. Type the rough idea as you would say it out loud — "a logo for a coffee shop" is enough — and it expands that into a structured prompt with the subject, style and constraints spelled out. Try it: AI Prompt Generator.

Text Summarizer — compress long text without losing the facts

Best for condensing an article, a long email thread, or a set of notes before you decide whether to read the whole thing. Choose a length based on what you need: short for a quick gist, medium for a working summary, long when the source covers several distinct points you want preserved. The summarizer is instructed to stick to facts already in your text rather than add anything new, but always skim the original before acting on anything important. Try it: Text Summarizer.

Text Rewriter — change the tone, keep the meaning

Useful when the content is right but the voice is wrong — a blunt message that needs softening before you send it, or a casual draft that needs to sound more professional for work. Pick the closest tone from the list and paste in a complete thought (a full paragraph works better than a single fragment, since the rewriter has more context to preserve). Try it: Text Rewriter.

Social Media Caption Generator — get three real options, not one guess

Describe the photo or post in a sentence and pick a platform; you get three captions with different angles (direct, playful, and a question-based hook) rather than a single output you have to accept or reject outright. None of them include hashtags automatically — add your own, since good hashtags depend on your niche and change over time in ways a generic tool can't track. Try it: Social Media Caption Generator.

AI Email Generator — a complete first draft, subject line included

Good for messages you know you need to send but keep putting off writing — asking for something, following up, or delivering news you'd rather soften. Describe the situation and the outcome you want, pick a tone, and you get a subject line plus a full body you can edit. It won't know details it hasn't been told, so name any specifics (dates, names, amounts) in your description rather than leaving them for the AI to invent. Try it: AI Email Generator.

Blog Outline Generator — a structure to write from, not a finished draft

Use this at the start of writing something, not the end. Give it a topic and an audience level, and it returns a title plus ordered sections with sub-points — a skeleton you fill in with your own research and voice. Treating the output as a finished article rather than a starting structure is the main way people get disappointed by this kind of tool. Try it: Blog Outline Generator.

A few things true of all six

  • Each tool sends your text to a secure server, which forwards it to the AI provider to generate a response. Nothing is stored afterward.
  • Each has a 6,000-character input limit (500 for the blog topic field) to keep responses fast; a live counter under the input shows how much room you have left.
  • None of them are a substitute for checking the result yourself — treat the output as a strong first draft, not a final answer, especially for anything with names, dates, or numbers in it.
