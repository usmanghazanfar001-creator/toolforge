How to Calculate CGPA

By ToolForge AI · 2026-09-28 · 2 min read

CGPA is a weighted average: courses with more credit hours count more. The formula is CGPA = total quality points ÷ total credit hours, where quality points = credit hours × grade points.

Worked example (4.0 scale)

CourseCreditsGradePointsCredits×Points
Calculus3A4.012.0
Physics4B+3.313.2
English2B3.06.0

Total quality points = 31.2, total credits = 9, so CGPA = 31.2 ÷ 9 = 3.47.

Tips

  • Check your university's grade scale; some use different point values.
  • For several semesters, add all courses together instead of averaging semester GPAs.

Planning a target CGPA

Work backwards. If you have completed 60 credit hours with a CGPA of 3.20 and want 3.40 after 90 credits, you need 90 × 3.40 = 306 total quality points. You already have 60 × 3.20 = 192, so the next 30 credits must earn 114 points, an average of 3.80. Seeing the number early shows whether a goal is realistic.

Common mistakes

  • Averaging semester GPAs. Semesters with more credits count for more, so average quality points, not GPAs.
  • Ignoring credit hours. A 4-credit course affects your result more than a 1-credit lab.
  • Using the wrong scale. Some universities use 4.0, others 5.0, 10-point or percentage systems. Match your institution’s table.
  • Mishandling repeated courses. Some schools replace the old grade, others average both. Check the policy.

Frequently asked questions

Do withdrawn or pass/fail courses count? Usually not, because they carry no grade points, but policies differ.

How can I raise my CGPA quickly? Focus on high-credit courses, since they move the average most, and retake courses if your university allows grade replacement.
