About the Prime Factorization

Every integer greater than 1 is either prime or can be written as a unique product of primes. Enter a number and this tool returns its prime factorization in both expanded and exponent form, and counts how many divisors it has. Prime factors are the basis for finding the GCD and LCM, simplifying fractions and understanding the number theory behind modern encryption.

When to use it

  • Finding the GCD or LCM of two numbers using their prime factors.
  • Simplifying fractions and radicals in algebra.
  • Working out how many divisors a number has.
💡 Pro tip

You only need to test divisors up to the square root of the number. If none divide it, the number is prime.

Frequently asked questions

What is a prime number?

A prime is a number greater than 1 whose only divisors are 1 and itself, such as 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11.

Is the factorization unique?

Yes. The fundamental theorem of arithmetic says every integer above 1 has exactly one prime factorization, ignoring order.

How do you count divisors from prime factors?

Add 1 to each exponent and multiply the results. For 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5, the count is 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 divisors.
