About the GCD Calculator

The greatest common divisor (GCD) is the largest integer that divides two numbers without a remainder. This calculator uses the Euclidean algorithm, repeatedly dividing and keeping the remainder, and displays each line of the working. It also finds the least common multiple and expresses the result as a linear combination ax + by (Bézout's identity), which is a standard requirement in number theory courses and modular arithmetic.

When to use it

  • Simplifying a fraction to lowest terms.
  • Checking homework on the Euclidean algorithm and Bézout coefficients.
  • Finding modular inverses, which rely on the extended Euclidean algorithm.
💡 Pro tip

Two numbers are coprime when their GCD is 1. That is the condition for a modular inverse to exist, which matters in cryptography such as RSA.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Euclidean algorithm?

Divide the larger number by the smaller, replace the larger with the smaller and the smaller with the remainder, and repeat until the remainder is 0. The last non-zero remainder is the GCD.

How is LCM related to GCD?

For two positive integers, LCM(a, b) = a × b ÷ GCD(a, b).

What does gcd(a, b) = ax + by mean?

Bézout's identity says the GCD can always be written as an integer combination of a and b. The calculator finds suitable x and y by working the algorithm backwards.
