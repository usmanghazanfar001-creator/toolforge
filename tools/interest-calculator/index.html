About the Simple & Compound Interest Calculator

Interest is the cost of borrowing or the reward for saving. Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal, while compound interest is calculated on the principal plus the interest already earned, so it grows faster over time. This calculator handles both, shows the formula with your numbers substituted, and reports the interest and final balance. It makes the power of compounding easy to see.

When to use it

  • Comparing how a savings deposit grows under simple versus compound interest.
  • Estimating the interest on a short-term loan.
  • Doing finance and math homework with the working shown.
💡 Pro tip

Compounding frequency matters. Interest compounded monthly grows slightly faster than interest compounded yearly at the same stated rate.

Frequently asked questions

What is the simple interest formula?

I = P × R × T ÷ 100, where P is the principal, R is the annual rate in percent and T is time in years.

What is the compound interest formula?

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the compounding periods per year and t is time in years.

Which grows faster?

Compound interest, because you earn interest on previously earned interest. The gap widens the longer the time period.
