About the Roman Numeral Converter

Roman numerals still appear on clocks, book chapters, movie sequels and monument dates. This converter turns an ordinary number into its Roman form and decodes Roman numerals into numbers, following the standard subtractive rules such as IV for 4 and IX for 9. It is handy for homework, design work and anyone decoding a copyright year on an old building.

When to use it

  • Writing a year or chapter number in Roman numerals.
  • Decoding numerals on a clock, building or document.
  • Checking Roman numeral homework.
💡 Pro tip

No symbol repeats more than three times in a row. 4 is IV, not IIII, and 40 is XL, not XXXX.

Frequently asked questions

What are the seven Roman numeral symbols?

I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100, D = 500 and M = 1000.

What is the largest number you can write?

Standard notation reaches 3999 (MMMCMXCIX). Larger values need an overline notation not covered here.

How does subtractive notation work?

A smaller symbol before a larger one is subtracted, for example IX = 9 and CM = 900.
